# Anticipated Questions & Answers

> What the firm will likely ask, why they ask it, and how to answer. **General rule:** give short
> answers, don't volunteer assets, steer back to the settlement number. You don't have to lie — and
> you shouldn't — but you're not required to give them a financial interview.

---

### "Why can't you pay the full balance?"
> "Because I genuinely don't have it. I've had a hard stretch financially. What I'm offering is money
> I've pulled together — partly from family — specifically to resolve this. It's the most I can do."

*Why they ask:* to test how firm you are. Don't flinch; repeat that the offer is your real limit.

---

### "Where is the money coming from?"
> "Personal savings and some help from family. I'd rather put it toward settling this than anything
> else." *(Do not say "the house" or "a home sale.")*

---

### "Do you own a home / real estate? Are you selling property?"
They already liened the house, and you need a release for a sale/refi — so acknowledge that much,
but **don't volunteer the closing date or how much you'll net.**
> "There's a possible sale or refinance — which is exactly why I want your lien **released**. But
> after the mortgage and my homestead exemption there's little equity behind it, so let's settle on a
> realistic release number rather than a financial interview."

**Never state anything false.** You're not hiding the lien (it's public record); you're just not
handing them your timing or proceeds.

---

### "We have a judgment lien on your house — why would we discount?"
This is the heart of it. Answer calmly with homestead + bankruptcy:
> "Because the lien isn't worth its face value to you. The house is jointly owned, these are only my
> debts, and after the mortgage and my **$182,625 Ohio homestead exemption** there's little or no
> equity you could actually reach on a sale. And in a Chapter 7, a judgment lien like this can be
> **stripped** where it impairs my homestead exemption. A discounted payoff to release the lien now
> is genuinely more than you'd net otherwise."

*Why it matters:* a recorded lien gives them some leverage (you need it released to close), but the
homestead exemption + § 522(f) lien-stripping caps what it's really worth. That's your counter.

---

### "How and when do we get paid?"
> "Through the closing — the payoff comes out of the transaction, and in exchange you deliver a
> recordable release of the lien and a satisfaction. I can't pay before then, and I can't pay without
> a signed agreement, but the release is what lets the deal fund."

---

### "What's your income / are you employed? Where do you work?"
> "I'd prefer to keep the financial interview out of this and just talk settlement. That's why I
> called you proactively rather than waiting."

*Why they ask:* to gauge garnishment potential. You're not obligated to answer a collector's
employment questions on the phone.

---

### "Where do you bank? What's your account number?"
> "I'll be paying by **cashier's check** once we have a signed agreement, so you won't need bank
> details." **Never give account/routing numbers or debit authorization.**

---

### "Are you married? Is your spouse liable / can your spouse pay?"
> "These are my individual accounts. I'm making this offer on my own. Let's keep it to these two
> judgments." *(The AmEx debts are yours alone; Olivia isn't on them. Don't get pulled into
> discussing her finances or the marriage.)*

---

### "Are you going to file bankruptcy?"
This one is **leverage** — use it calmly, don't threaten.
> "I'm trying hard to avoid that, which is exactly why I'm calling to settle voluntarily. But
> realistically it's the alternative if I can't resolve these — and in that case the recovery here
> would likely be nothing. I'd rather pay you something real today."

---

### "We can't go that low — the most we can discount is X%."
> "I understand. This is borrowed money and it's my ceiling. What's the lowest number that closes
> out **both** accounts today with a satisfaction filed?" *(Then move up slowly toward your target.)*

---

### "We can do a payment plan."
> "If a lump-sum discount isn't possible, I'd consider a short plan — around **40% over 60–90
> days** — but only if you agree **in writing** not to file a certificate of judgment or pursue
> collection while I'm paying, and you file the satisfaction once it's done."

*Reminder:* a lump sum gets the biggest discount. Prefer it if you can fund it.

---

### "We'll email you something / just pay over the phone today."
> "I'm glad to move fast, but I can't send any money until I have the **signed agreement** in hand.
> Send me the written terms and I'll turn the payment around quickly."

**Never pay before you have a signed agreement.** This is non-negotiable.

---

### "Will this hurt / help your credit?"
Fair to ask them to confirm reporting in writing:
> "I'd want the agreement to state both accounts will be reported as **settled/satisfied with a $0
> balance**." *(A satisfied judgment is better than an open one; get it in writing.)*

---

### Things THEY may not mention that you should know
- **Taxes (1099-C):** Forgiven debt over $600 can be reported to the IRS as income. If they forgive
  ~$28k+ across both, you may get 1099-C forms. You may be able to exclude it if **insolvent** at the
  time (IRS Form 982) — ask a tax pro. Budget for this possibility; don't let it surprise you.
- **Satisfaction of Judgment is the finish line — not the payment.** The case isn't truly closed
  until the **file-stamped satisfaction** is on the Hamilton County docket. Verify it yourself.
- **Get the rep's name and the deal in writing every time.** Verbal collection promises are not
  enforceable.

---

## Your one-sentence anchors (memorize these)
- "After the mortgage and my homestead exemption, your lien reaches little or nothing on a sale."
- "A judgment lien like this can be stripped in Chapter 7 — a discounted release now beats that."
- "I want your lien **released** for a realistic number so I can clear title."
- "I can't send any money until I have a signed agreement."
- "I'd rather not get into a financial interview or a hard date." *(deflect timing/proceeds)*
