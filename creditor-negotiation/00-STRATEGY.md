## The 3 strategic calls (my recommendation)

1. **Objective: discounted lump sum + a filed _Satisfaction of Judgment_, done before the house
   closes.** The judgments are NOT yet liens on your house. The win is to wipe them out so they
   can never attach — not just to "make a payment."

2. **Disclosure: minimal. Do NOT volunteer the house sale or the divorce.** If they learn money is
   coming, they'll race to file a certificate of judgment (which creates the lien) and hold out
   for full payment. Frame your money as "what I can scrape together — some savings, some help from
   family," **not** "from the house." You don't have to lie; you just don't volunteer it.

3. **Timing: settle (and ideally pay) before closing**, so the satisfaction is filed and title is
   clean. If you must wait for funds, the written deal must include a **standstill** (they won't
   file a certificate of judgment / won't pursue collection while you perform).

## Why you have leverage (true things you can say)

- **No lien yet.** In Ohio a judgment only becomes a lien on your house when the creditor files a
  *certificate of judgment* with the Hamilton County Clerk of Common Pleas (R.C. 2329.02). They
  haven't. Collecting on an unsecured judgment is slow and uncertain — a clean lump sum now is
  worth more to them than a bigger number they may never collect.
- **The house is a hard target for them.** It's owned jointly by you **and Olivia**. These AmEx
  debts are **yours alone**, so a lien could only attach to **your half-interest** — not Olivia's.
  And Ohio's **homestead exemption protects $182,625** of your equity (R.C. 2329.66). With a
  $311,756 mortgage on a home insured around $524,500, your exposed, non-exempt share is small to
  nonexistent. Translation: even if they lien it, they likely get little. *(Confirm specifics with
  the attorney.)*
- **Bankruptcy is your alternative — and theirs to fear.** If they won't be reasonable, a Chapter 7
  could discharge these balances and they'd get **$0**. You can say this calmly: "I'm trying to
  resolve this voluntarily instead of filing."
- **Dormancy clock.** An Ohio judgment goes dormant after 5 years without execution (R.C. 2329.07).

## IRON RULES — do not break these

1. **Get the agreement in writing and signed BEFORE you pay one dollar.** A verbal promise is
   worthless. No signed agreement = no payment.
2. **The written agreement must say all of this:**
   - the settlement dollar amount and that it **fully satisfies** the judgment(s), by case number;
   - the firm will **file a Satisfaction of Judgment within ~30 days** of payment clearing;
   - the firm will **not file or record a certificate of judgment** and will **release/cancel any
     lien** if one exists;
   - **no further collection** on these accounts;
   - the credit bureaus will be updated to **"settled"/"satisfied," $0 balance**;
   - if you pay over time, a **standstill**: no certificate of judgment / no execution while you pay.
3. **Protect your bank account.** Pay by **cashier's check or money order**. Do **NOT** give them
   your debit/checking access, and do **NOT** authorize an auto-draft of the full balance.
4. **Keep proof.** Get a **receipt**, and later the **file-stamped Satisfaction of Judgment** from
   the court. Save every email.
5. **You may record the call** — Ohio is a one-party-consent state (R.C. 2933.52). At minimum write
   down the rep's name, the date/time, and exactly what was agreed.
6. **Don't drain your cash on the small judgments first.** See the next box.

## Two cautions (why the attorney consult matters)

- **Fraudulent transfer.** Selling the house and moving the proceeds while these creditors are
  unpaid can be unwound under Ohio's Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act (R.C. 1336) if it looks like
  an attempt to hinder/delay/defraud creditors. Settling the debts is the clean fix; do it with
  professional guidance on timing.
- **Dissolution disclosure.** Ohio dissolution requires **sworn financial affidavits**. Not
  volunteering the sale to a collector is fine; **lying under oath or hiding assets in the divorce
  is not.** Coordinate the debt payoff with how the decree allocates these debts.
