# Call Script — LVNV Funding (Best Egg) Lawsuits (read aloud)

> Speak slowly and calmly. You are not in trouble; **you're the one offering them money to make two
> lawsuits go away.** Pauses are good — after you state a number, **stop talking** and let them respond.
>
> Firm: **Stenger & Stenger, P.C.** — call **(877) 375-4220** (Defendant/Counsel line). Ask for the
> **settlement department**. Case numbers: **A2505329** and **A2505276** (Stenger file #s 25-232123
> and 25-232156). Fill in `[REP NAME]` once you have it. Have a pen ready to log the rep's name,
> time, and what's agreed.
>
> **Before you dial:** confirm on the docket whether you were served and whether any answer deadline
> passed (see 00-STRATEGY "Do this first"). If a default could be looming, loop in an attorney first.

---

## 0) Before they pick up — your one-line goal
> "Settle both LVNV lawsuits (~**$11k–$25k**) for a one-time lump sum in exchange for **dismissal of
> both cases WITH PREJUDICE and no judgment**, paid through closing under a **standstill**, with
> **nothing paid until I have a signed agreement.**"

---

## 1) Opening

> "Hi, my name is **Jeffrey Boeh**. Your firm filed two lawsuits against me on behalf of LVNV
> Funding — Hamilton County case numbers **A2505329** and **A2505276**, your file numbers
> **25-232123** and **25-232156**. I'd like to talk with someone about **resolving both of them
> before this goes any further**. Who handles settlements?"

When you reach the right person, get their name:
> "Before we start — can I get your name and a direct number or extension, in case we get cut off?"
> *(Write it down.)*

---

## 2) Make them prove they own the debt (validation leverage)

> "First thing — these were Best Egg loans originally with Cross River Bank, and your complaint says
> LVNV is the assignee, but I didn't see any actual proof of that. Before I pay anyone, I'll want to
> see that **LVNV actually owns these accounts** — the full chain of assignment from Cross River Bank
> and the account records. That said, I'd genuinely rather **resolve this than fight about it**, if we
> can be reasonable."

*(This plants the standing problem and signals you know it — then you pivot to settling.)*

---

## 3) Set the frame (hardship, voluntary, lump sum)

> "I'll be straight with you. I've had a rough financial stretch and I'm trying to clean things up. I
> can't pay these in full — that's not realistic. But I've pulled together some money, partly from
> family, and I'd rather use it to **settle both of these voluntarily** than have everyone fight over
> scraps. I'm hoping we can agree on a **one-time lump-sum settlement** on both cases."

*(Keep it short. Don't over-explain. Do not mention the house sale or the divorce.)*

---

## 4) The opening offer

> "For both cases together — A2505329 and A2505276 — I can offer a **lump sum of $11,000** to fully
> resolve both, in exchange for **dismissing both cases with prejudice and taking no judgment**. It
> would fund from a sale or refinance in the works, so it would **pay through closing**, but I'll
> commit to a firm timeline."

**Then stop talking.** Let the silence sit. They will almost always counter.

---

## 4b) The leverage (use it the moment they push on the number)

> "Let's be realistic about what you'd actually collect here. You'd first have to prove LVNV owns
> these loans — and there's a **binding arbitration clause** in the Best Egg agreement that, as the
> assignee, you're bound by. On top of that, the house is owned with my spouse, these are only my
> debts, and after the mortgage and my **Ohio homestead exemption** there's little or no equity a
> judgment could reach — and a judgment lien like that can be **stripped in Chapter 7**. So a
> reasonable lump sum now to dismiss both cases is genuinely better for you than what you'd net
> chasing a judgment."

---

## 5) Branches — how to respond

### A) "That's far too low."
> "I understand it's lower than you'd like. But this is essentially **borrowed money** — it's the
> most I can put my hands on. Realistically, what's the lowest you can take to make **both** of these
> go away with a dismissal with prejudice?"

Step up **slowly** — $14k… $18k… toward your $18k–$25k target. Never jump to your max.
> "I might be able to get to **$18,000** if that **dismisses both cases with prejudice** and there's
> no judgment. That's a real stretch for me."

### B) "We need the full balance."
> "Full payment isn't something I can do — if I could, I wouldn't be calling. And realistically: you'd
> have to prove LVNV owns these, you're bound by the loan's arbitration clause, and even with a
> judgment my house is jointly owned, these are only my debts, and my homestead exemption plus a
> Chapter 7 lien-strip mean you'd likely recover **very little**. A discounted lump sum to dismiss
> both now is worth far more to you than the number on paper."

### C) "Do you own a home? / Are you selling? / Any property?"
You can acknowledge a sale/refi is the funding source, but **don't volunteer the closing date or how
much you'll net.**
> "There's a possible sale or refinance — that's where the money would come from. But there's not much
> equity behind it after the mortgage and my homestead exemption, so let's settle on a realistic
> number rather than a financial interview. What's the lowest you'll take to dismiss both with
> prejudice?"

If they push for financials/timing:
> "I'd rather not get into an itemized financial interview or a hard date. The point is a judgment
> nets you little here and could be stripped in bankruptcy — so let's land on a settlement number."

*(Never state anything false; you're just not volunteering timing or proceeds.)*

### D) "What do you make? / Where do you bank? / Where's the money coming from?"
> "Personal savings and family help. I'd rather keep the financial interview out of it and just talk
> settlement numbers — that's why I called instead of waiting for you to chase it."
**Never give bank/routing numbers or debit authorization.**

### E) They get aggressive / threaten a judgment or garnishment.
Stay calm.
> "You're welcome to pursue whatever remedies you think you have — I understand that. But you'd have
> to prove standing, deal with the arbitration clause, and then a judgment still runs into my
> homestead exemption and a possible bankruptcy lien-strip. I'm trying to save us both the time and
> expense by settling today. So — can we agree on a lump sum to dismiss both, yes or no?"

### F) They'll only do a payment plan, not a deep discount.
> "If a deep lump-sum discount isn't possible, I'd consider a **short** structured deal — say
> **40% over 60 to 90 days** — but only if the agreement says you'll **take no default judgment and
> no other action while I'm paying**, and you **dismiss both cases with prejudice** once it's paid."

### G) They verbally accept.
> "Great. To make sure we're on the same page: **$[AGREED] total**, paid by cashier's check at
> closing, that **fully resolves both** A2505329 and A2505276; upon payment you'll **dismiss both
> cases WITH PREJUDICE and take no judgment**; you'll take **no default or other action** in the
> meantime; you'll report both to the credit bureaus as **settled, zero balance**; and there's **no
> further collection**. Right?"

---

## 6) Close — the most important part

> "Perfect. Please **put all of that in writing and email it to me** — the settlement amount, the
> **dismissal of both cases with prejudice**, that no judgment will be taken, the standstill, and the
> credit reporting. Once I have the **signed agreement** I'll coordinate payment through closing. To
> be clear — **I can't send any money until I have the signed agreement in hand.** What email will it
> come from, and how soon can you send it?"

Confirm and log:

- Agreed amount: $__________  •  Paid by/through: __________ (closing)
- **Dismissal WITH prejudice of BOTH cases** confirmed?  ☐ Yes  •  No judgment to be taken?  ☐ Yes
- Standstill (no default motion) confirmed?  ☐ Yes
- They'll email the agreement from: ____________________  •  By (date): __________

> "Thanks for working with me on this. I'll watch for that email today."

---

## Do-NOT list (keep visible)
- ❌ Don't give bank account/routing or debit authorization.
- ❌ Don't agree to an auto-draft.
- ❌ Don't pay anything before a **signed** agreement.
- ❌ Don't accept a deal that doesn't say **"dismissed WITH prejudice"** and **"no judgment."**
- ❌ Don't volunteer the closing date, your net proceeds, or the divorce. (You can say a sale/refi is
  the funding source — just not the timing or the numbers.)
- ❌ Don't lie — if cornered on a direct question, **redirect**, don't fabricate.
- ❌ Don't let them rush you. "I need to see it in writing first" ends any pressure.
