## ⚠️ DO THIS FIRST — protect against a default judgment

These were filed in **late October 2025** and are **still pending with no judgment**. That usually
means **service was never perfected** *or* LVNV simply hasn't moved for default yet. Before you get
comfortable negotiating:

1. **Pull the live docket** for both case numbers at the Hamilton County Clerk of Courts
   (clerkofcourts.hamilton-co.org). Look for: a **returned/served summons** (and its date), any
   **answer deadline**, and any **motion for default judgment**.
2. **If you were served and the answer window (≈28 days) has passed**, LVNV can ask for a default
   judgment at any time — which would turn this into the same lien problem as AmEx. In that case
   **call a consumer-debt attorney today** to file a short **protective answer** (it preserves your
   defenses, including arbitration, and stops a quiet default) *before or alongside* negotiating.
3. **If you were never properly served**, you have time and leverage — but don't bank on it; the
   clock can start the moment they re-serve.

> The whole point: **never let a default judgment slip in while you're negotiating.** A signed
> standstill (below) or a filed answer is what protects you.

## Why you have leverage (true things you can say)

- **They haven't shown they own the debt.** Their complaint calls LVNV the "assignee," but their
  own Exhibit A is only the **Best Egg loan agreement and statements** — there is **no bill of sale
  or chain of assignment** from Cross River Bank to LVNV. A debt buyer has to prove the complete
  chain of title. **Demand it in writing** (debt validation). Many debt buyers can't produce a clean
  chain — which pushes them toward a cheap settlement or even a dismissal.
- **There's a binding arbitration clause** (paragraph 26 of the loan agreement they attached). LVNV,
  as assignee, is bound by it, and you almost certainly never opted out in 2022. **Electing
  arbitration (AAA/JAMS) is slow and expensive for a debt buyer** and a strong reason for them to
  settle. *(This is really an attorney's move — raise it on your consult.)*
- **A judgment would barely reach you anyway.** The house is jointly owned with Olivia; these are
  **your** debts only, so any future judgment lien attaches to **your ~half-interest** only, and
  Ohio's **$182,625 homestead exemption** (R.C. 2329.66) sits on top. After the **$311,756 mortgage**
  there's little or no non-exempt equity to reach — and in **Chapter 7 a judgment lien can be
  stripped** where it impairs your homestead (11 U.S.C. § 522(f)). So "we'll just get a judgment"
  isn't worth much to them.
- **Bankruptcy is your alternative — and theirs to fear.** Across ~$142k of judgments + pending
  claims, a Chapter 7 could discharge most of this and they'd net **≈ $0**. You can say calmly: "I'm
  trying to resolve this voluntarily instead of filing."

2. **The written agreement must say all of this:**

3. **Protect your bank account.** Pay by **cashier's check or money order**. Do **NOT** give them
   debit/checking access, and do **NOT** authorize an auto-draft.
4. **Keep proof.** Get a **receipt**, and later confirm the **file-stamped dismissal-with-prejudice
   entries** on the Hamilton County docket for both case numbers. Save every email.
5. **You may record the call** — Ohio is a one-party-consent state (R.C. 2933.52). At minimum write
   down the rep's name, the date/time, and exactly what was agreed.
6. **Don't drain your cash** on these before an attorney sizes up the whole picture (next box).

## Two cautions (why the attorney consult matters)

- **Fraudulent transfer.** Selling the house and moving the proceeds while these creditors are
  unpaid can be unwound under Ohio's Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act (R.C. 1336) if it looks like an
  attempt to hinder/delay/defraud creditors. Settling the debts is the clean fix; do it with
  professional guidance on timing.
- **Dissolution disclosure.** Ohio dissolution requires **sworn financial affidavits**. Not
  volunteering the sale to a collector is fine; **lying under oath or hiding assets in the divorce is
  not.** Coordinate the debt payoff with how the decree allocates these debts.
