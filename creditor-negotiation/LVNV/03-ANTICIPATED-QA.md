# Anticipated Questions & Answers — LVNV Funding (Best Egg) Lawsuits

> What the firm will likely ask, why they ask it, and how to answer. **General rule:** give short
> answers, don't volunteer assets, steer back to the settlement number and the dismissal. You don't
> have to lie — and you shouldn't — but you're not required to give a collector a financial interview.

---

### "Do you dispute the debt?"
Don't admit or deny the dollar amount; turn it into your validation lever.
> "I want to see that LVNV actually owns these accounts — the full chain of assignment from Cross
> River Bank and the account records. That said, I'd rather resolve both cases than litigate, if the
> number's reasonable."

*Why it matters:* their Exhibit A has **no bill of sale** proving LVNV owns the loans. Making them
prove standing is leverage.

---

### "Why can't you pay the full balance?"
> "Because I genuinely don't have it. I've had a hard stretch financially. What I'm offering is money
> I've pulled together — partly from family — specifically to resolve this. It's the most I can do."

*Why they ask:* to test how firm you are. Don't flinch; repeat that the offer is your real limit.

---

### "Where is the money coming from?"
> "Personal savings and some help from family. I'd rather put it toward settling this than anything
> else." *(You can acknowledge a possible sale/refi is the source if pressed — but do not give the
> closing date or how much you'll net.)*

---

### "Do you own a home / real estate? Are you selling property?"
> "There's a possible sale or refinance — that's where the money would come from. But after the
> mortgage and my homestead exemption there's little equity behind it, so let's settle on a realistic
> number rather than a financial interview."

**Never state anything false.** You're just not handing them your timing or proceeds.

---

### "We'll just get a judgment — why would we discount?"
This is the heart of it. Answer calmly with standing + arbitration + homestead:
> "A few reasons. First, you'd have to prove LVNV actually owns these loans — your complaint doesn't
> attach the assignment. Second, the Best Egg agreement has a **binding arbitration clause** you're
> bound by as the assignee. And third, even with a judgment: the house is jointly owned, these are
> only my debts, and after the mortgage and my **$182,625 Ohio homestead exemption** there's little
> or no equity you could reach — plus a judgment lien like that can be **stripped in Chapter 7**. A
> reasonable lump sum to dismiss both cases now is genuinely more than you'd net otherwise."

---

### "How and when do we get paid?"
> "Through the closing — the money comes out of a sale or refinance, and in exchange you dismiss both
> cases with prejudice and take no judgment. I can't pay before then, and I can't pay without a signed
> agreement, but I'll commit to a firm deadline."

---

### "What's your income / are you employed? Where do you work?"
> "I'd prefer to keep the financial interview out of this and just talk settlement. That's why I
> called you proactively rather than waiting."

*Why they ask:* to gauge garnishment potential after a judgment. You're not obligated to answer a
collector's employment questions on the phone.

---

### "Where do you bank? What's your account number?"
> "I'll be paying by **cashier's check** once we have a signed agreement, so you won't need bank
> details." **Never give account/routing numbers or debit authorization.**

---

### "Are you married? Is your spouse liable / can your spouse pay?"
> "These are my individual loans. I'm making this offer on my own. Let's keep it to these two cases."
> *(The Best Egg loans are yours alone; Olivia isn't on them. Don't get pulled into discussing her
> finances or the marriage.)*

---

### "Are you going to file bankruptcy?"
This one is **leverage** — use it calmly, don't threaten.
> "I'm trying hard to avoid that, which is exactly why I'm calling to settle voluntarily. But
> realistically it's the alternative if I can't resolve these — and in that case the recovery here
> would likely be nothing. I'd rather pay you something real today."

---

### "We can't go that low — the most we can discount is X%."
> "I understand. This is borrowed money and it's my ceiling. What's the lowest number that closes out
> **both** cases today with a dismissal with prejudice?" *(Then move up slowly toward your target.)*

---

### "We can do a payment plan."
> "If a lump-sum discount isn't possible, I'd consider a short plan — around **40% over 60–90 days** —
> but only if you agree **in writing** to take no default judgment and no other action while I'm
> paying, and you dismiss both cases with prejudice once it's done."

*Reminder:* a lump sum gets the biggest discount. Prefer it if you can fund it.

---

### "We'll email you something / just pay over the phone today."
> "I'm glad to move fast, but I can't send any money until I have the **signed agreement** in hand.
> Send me the written terms and I'll turn the payment around quickly."

**Never pay before you have a signed agreement.** This is non-negotiable.

---

### "Will this hurt / help your credit?"
> "I'd want the agreement to state both accounts will be reported as **settled with a $0 balance**."
> *(A settled, dismissed case is better than an open lawsuit or a judgment; get it in writing.)*

---

### Things THEY may not mention that you should know
- **Don't get defaulted.** These are live lawsuits. Before relying on a verbal "we'll hold off,"
  confirm you weren't already served past your answer deadline — and get the **standstill in
  writing**. If a default could be looming, have an attorney file a short protective answer.
- **Dismissal WITH prejudice is the finish line — not the payment.** The cases aren't truly closed
  until the **file-stamped dismissal-with-prejudice entries** are on the Hamilton County docket for
  **both** case numbers. Verify it yourself. ("With prejudice" = they can't refile.)
- **Taxes (1099-C):** Forgiven debt over $600 can be reported to the IRS as income. If they forgive
  ~$45k+ across both, you may get 1099-C forms. You may be able to exclude it if **insolvent** at the
  time (IRS Form 982) — ask a tax pro. Don't let it surprise you.
- **Get the rep's name and the deal in writing every time.** Verbal collection promises are not
  enforceable.

---

## Your one-sentence anchors (memorize these)
- "Show me the assignment — prove LVNV actually owns these loans."
- "There's a binding arbitration clause you're bound by as the assignee."
- "Even with a judgment, my homestead exemption leaves you little to reach — and it's strippable in Chapter 7."
- "I want both cases **dismissed with prejudice**, with no judgment."
- "I can't send any money until I have a signed agreement."
- "I'd rather not get into a financial interview or a hard date." *(deflect timing/proceeds)*
